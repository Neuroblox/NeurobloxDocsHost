Terminology

Simulating a brain circuit involves several things that are easy to conflate: the connectivity one writes down, the graph one parameterizes it into, the particular instantiation a build of that graph produces, the state it has been brought into, and the test one then runs on it. This page names them and says how they relate.

TermWhat it is
CircuitThe connectivity of the top-level graph — which blox are present and which connections exist between them, and what they mean biologically.
PopulationA Circuit together with the parameter values it carries: the graph one constructs, and what every Individual is drawn from.
IndividualOne instantiation of a Population: its flat graph, with every composite expanded and every wiring rule resolved.
EnsembleA group of Individuals of one Population, each simulated alike and summarized across the group.
Condition ProfileThe persistent background conditions an Individual carries — physiological state, learning history, prior exposure, lasting effects of past interventions.
PreparationThe set of simulations and transformations that produces a Condition Profile.
InterventionAn externally applied manipulation, transient or persistent, expressed as an @intervention block.
TrialOne test: an Individual under a stimulus and possibly an intervention, observed.
ExperimentA coherent set of Trials answering one question.

Populations, Individuals, and Ensembles

A Population does not describe one graph. Each time it is built, the internals of its composite blox and any connection stated statistically rather than edge by edge are drawn afresh, so building the same Population twice can give two different wirings. A DensityRule between two composite blox, for instance, includes each possible connection between their neurons independently with probability p: the density is what the Population specifies, and which neurons end up wired to which is decided anew on every build. A Population is therefore the procedure that produces Individuals, not one of them.

An Individual is one such instantiation, under a seed. The definition identifies the Population and the seed identifies the draw, so an Individual is named by the pair without having to be kept. The Individuals of a Population are simply the instantiations sharing its definition; there is no separate word for them as a set, and a second draw of twenty is twenty more Individuals of the same Population.

The distinction is the one between a graph and its flat graph, described in the connection type system. A Population is the graph one constructs, where a composite blox stands for its contents and a wiring rule stands for a connectivity pattern. An Individual is the flat graph that building it produces, where every composite has been expanded into the simple blox it holds and every wiring rule has been resolved into the particular edges it drew.

A Circuit is broader than either. It is the connectivity of that top-level graph — which blox are present, what connects to what, and what those connections mean biologically — independent of the values attached to them. Two Populations that give the same blox different parameter values are two parameterizations of one Circuit.

An Ensemble is a group of Individuals drawn from one Population. The same simulation is run on each of them and the results are reported as statistics over the group, so what an Ensemble measures is the spread a Population produces rather than the behavior of any one Individual.

Condition Profiles and Preparations

Two Individuals of the same Population may still not be interchangeable: one may be naive and the other conditioned, one drug-naive and the other chronically dosed. A Condition Profile names that difference — the persistent state an Individual is in, as opposed to whatever a single Trial does to it.

A Preparation is what puts an Individual into a condition: a learning run, a period of exposure, solving until it settles and keeping the state it settled into, or an assignment that needs no simulation at all. The profile records what the preparation did.

Interventions, Trials and Experiments

An Intervention is an externally applied manipulation — a dose, a lesion, a stimulus train — expressed as an @intervention block. It is transient if it lasts for the one Trial that applies it, and persistent if it is kept afterwards. A persistent intervention is a Preparation, and what it leaves behind is part of the Condition Profile.

A Trial is one test of an Individual in a given condition: a stimulus, optionally some interventions, and an observation. A Preparation changes what the Individual is; a Trial asks what it does. An Experiment is a set of Trials that together answer one question — a dose series, a comparison of conditions, a lesion against its control.
